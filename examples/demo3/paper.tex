\section{Introduction}

Standard latent diffusion models compress high-resolution RGB frames into compact spatial
representations before denoising, and a trajectory-consistent sampler then aligns the
velocity field with the rectified flow used during training. In our implementation the
sampler reuses the cached text embedding, so the extra cost is one forward pass per step
rather than a full re-encode of every frame and prompt pair. The remaining bottlenecks
are well understood and we list them explicitly, because each of them was measured on a
single GPU with the same batch size and the same random seed as the baseline, which makes
the comparison fair. This is why we believe the following three issues are the ones that
matter for long video.

\begin{itemize}
\item Quadratic attention matrix memory overhead across long video sequence lengths.
\item Redundant background patch representations degrade fine-grained local details.
\item Unstable gradient norms during end-to-end optimization with frozen encoders.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Sampling}
\noindent Choosing the map is an integer problem, and we relax it in the usual way: the
binary variable that says whether shard $s$ is placed in region $r$ becomes a fraction,
the constraint that every shard sits in at least three regions becomes a lower bound on
the sum, and the objective becomes linear in the relaxed variables. The relaxation is
tight in practice because the objective is dominated by a linear latency term, and the
rounded solution differs from the relaxed one on fewer than two percent of the shards.
Both rules are implemented as constraints in the solver rather than as post-hoc filters,
because a post-hoc filter can produce a map that is infeasible with respect to the
quorum constraint.
